Posting is fast: an idea, a few sentences, out into a feed that's already
moved on by the time you finish typing. Writing, the kind that holds up a
year later, is slower — and the web mostly isn't built for slow anymore.

A personal site with an RSS feed and no algorithm is one of the few formats
left that rewards taking the extra day. Nothing pushes a half-finished post
in front of readers before it's ready. Nothing punishes a piece for being
published a week after the news cycle that inspired it has passed.

## Editing is the actual work

The habit worth building isn't writing more — it's cutting more. A draft
that's twice as long as it needs to be isn't more thorough, it's just
unedited. The second read is where a post actually gets written; the first
draft is only the raw material.

## Publish when it's ready, not when it's timely

Field Notes has no publishing schedule for exactly this reason. A post goes
up when the argument holds together, not on a Tuesday because that's when
engagement is supposedly highest. That's a small luxury, and it's available
to anyone willing to own their own platform instead of renting someone
else's.
