A reader's focus is fragile. Typography on the web too often distracts rather
than assists, cycling through ornamental fonts, aggressive weights, and competing
hierarchies.

Functional typography begins with reading comfort. When line height, measure, and
proportions are balanced, the type becomes nearly invisible, leaving only the voice
of the writing.

## Two Typefaces Are Enough

In Tiny, we locked the design down to two complementary typefaces: Geist Sans
for body text and headings, and Geist Mono for dates, tags, and metadata.

> [!INFO] Choosing typographic scales
> Rather than arbitrary font sizes, a modular scale ensures every step feels proportional.
> With a base of $16\text{px}$ and a ratio $r = 1.25$ (a major third):
> 
> $$
> f_n = f_0 \cdot r^n
> $$
> 
> Each heading level naturally inherits a consistent visual weight.

As explored in [[On Intentional Software]], restraint is not the absence of craft—it
is the deliberate decision to stop when the purpose is met. You can see how this
hierarchy renders in [[A Markdown Showcase#Emphasis]].

Even visual essays like [[Autumn in Kyoto]] rely on subtle typographic rhythm to
frame photographs without competing with them.
