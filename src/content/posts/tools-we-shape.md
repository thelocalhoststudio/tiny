> We shape our tools and thereafter our tools shape us.
> — John Culkin (1967)

Every tool has an implicit worldview. A word processor with five hundred formatting
buttons encourages you to tweak margins and fonts instead of finding the right word.
A plain sheet of paper asks for nothing except your attention.

## Physical and Digital Boundaries

There is a tactile honesty in analog tools: a fountain pen has physical friction,
and a notebook has a fixed number of pages. You cannot endlessly scroll, re-theme,
or install a plugin when you run out of ideas.

| Medium | Friction | Lifespan | Primary distraction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Paper notebook | High (irreversible ink) | Decades | None |
| Plain text file | Low (fast editing) | Centuries | File organisation |
| Modern CMS | High (complex UI) | Years | Configuration fatigue |

> [!IMPORTANT] Creative constraints
> An interface that offers every possible formatting option paradoxically narrows
> original thought. When the boundary is fixed, creativity is forced outward into
> the ideas themselves.

When establishing [[Introducing Field Notes]], our goal was to bring that same
quiet boundary to the web. By pairing plain Markdown with [Lightweight Web Design](./lightweight-web-design.md),
publishing becomes as straightforward as filing a note.

This principle extends to storage: as discussed in [[local-first-software|local storage]],
the best tools remain accessible even when disconnected from the network.
