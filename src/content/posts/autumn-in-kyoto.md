Walking through Higashiyama before six in the morning is a different city.
The tour buses haven't arrived, shopkeepers are quietly hosing down the flagstones,
and the autumn mist still hangs between the cedar trees.

I carried one camera and a single 35mm prime lens for two weeks. When you can't
zoom, you stop thinking about focal lengths and start paying attention to where your
feet are placed. It reminds me of what I wrote in [[writing-on-the-web|writing slowly]]:
constraints reveal what actually matters.

## Fushimi in the Mist

Higher up the hill, above the torii gates, the crowd thins out completely. The
light comes in horizontally through the canopy, catching the moisture in the air.

![Stone lantern and raked gravel in a temple courtyard](/images/posts/kyoto-garden.svg "Courtyard stillness")

*Early morning light across Higashiyama and the meditative silence of temple stone courtyards.*

> [!TIP] Exposure and morning fog
> When photographing dense mist or dawn light, camera meters frequently underexpose
> to compensate for the bright haze. Overexpose by $+0.7$ to $+1.0$ EV to keep the
> whites luminous rather than muddy grey.

The temple gardens are raked each dawn before visitors enter. Every stone and
swirl of sand has an intentional place, echoing the principles in
[[A Markdown Showcase]] where typography and whitespace are given room to breathe.
